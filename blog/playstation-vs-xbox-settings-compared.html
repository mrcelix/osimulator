PlayStation vs Xbox settings, menu by menu

Sony and Microsoft solve the same problems with very different menu trees. Knowing which top-level heading owns a feature saves most of the hunting.

Getting into settings at all

On a PlayStation 5 the entry point is the gear icon in the top right of the home screen, which opens a long vertical list: Users and Accounts, Family and Parental Controls, Storage, Screen and Video, Sound, Accessibility, System, Network, and more.

On an Xbox Series X or S you press the Xbox button, move to Profile & system and choose Settings. The list is shorter because Microsoft groups aggressively: Account, General, Devices & connections, System, and Accessibility.

That grouping is the core difference. Sony gives many features their own top-level row, while Microsoft buries several inside General, including audio, display, power and network. The PlayStation vs Xbox comparison shows both trees together.

Accounts and family controls

PlayStation splits this in two. Users and Accounts handles sign-in, linked services and account switching, while Family and Parental Controls is a separate top-level entry for child accounts, spending limits and playtime.

Xbox keeps sign-in and privacy under Account, and files child settings under General, then Online safety & family. Microsoft also pushes much of this to the web and to the Family Safety mobile app, so some limits are set off-console entirely.

Both let you require a passcode or password at sign-in, which is the single most useful change on a shared console, along the lines described in the lock and security guide.

Storage and video output

Storage is a top-level PS5 item covering internal storage, an installed M.2 SSD and USB extended storage. Xbox files it under System, then Storage, covering internal, the proprietary expansion card and USB drives.

The rules are similar in spirit: current-generation titles need fast internal or expansion storage to run, while older-generation games can be played from a USB drive. Either way, clearing space is a recurring chore, and the storage guide covers the general approach.

For picture quality, PS5 uses Screen and Video, which holds resolution, HDR and a guided HDR adjustment. Xbox uses General, then TV & display options, which holds resolution, video modes and separate calibration tools for HDR games.

Accessibility, power and network

Accessibility is top-level on both, which is unusual and welcome. Each offers a screen reader, text size and contrast options, subtitle controls and controller button remapping.

Power differs in naming. PS5 groups rest mode behaviour and standby power under System, controlling whether the console keeps USB ports powered and downloads running. Xbox puts its power modes under General, then Power options, where the choice is essentially between fast resume and lower standby draw.

Network is top-level on PS5 and sits under General on Xbox. Both offer wired and wireless setup plus a connection test that reports NAT type, and the general steps match the Wi-Fi guide.

Which menu is easier to navigate

PlayStation is faster to scan. Because Storage, Network, Accessibility and Family each have their own row, you rarely need to guess where something was filed.

Xbox is tidier but requires more knowledge. Once you learn that General is the catch-all for audio, display, power and network, the tree becomes predictable, and Microsoft's search in the guide helps.

Neither is obviously better, and both change layout between system updates, so check your own dashboard version before following any tutorial. You can browse the actual menu structures for PlayStation and Xbox in the free browser simulator without a console in front of you.

Key points

  • PlayStation gives most features a top-level row; Xbox nests them under General.
  • HDR lives in Screen and Video on PS5, TV and display options on Xbox.
  • Accessibility is a top-level menu on both consoles.

Frequently asked questions

Can I play current-generation games from a USB drive?

Generally no. Both consoles allow current-generation titles to be stored on USB for transfer, but they must be moved back to internal or expansion storage to run. Older-generation titles can usually run from USB.

Where are HDR settings on each console?

PlayStation keeps them under Screen and Video, including a step-by-step HDR adjustment. Xbox keeps them under General, then TV and display options, with separate calibration for HDR games.

Which console has better parental controls?

Both are capable. PlayStation puts Family and Parental Controls at the top level of the console menu, while Xbox splits controls between the console and Microsoft Family Safety on the web and mobile.
