Where is the VPN setting on your device?

A VPN can be switched on from two completely different places: the operating system's own network settings, or inside the provider's app. Knowing which one you are looking at explains most of the confusion about whether a VPN is actually running.

iPhone and iPad: General, then VPN

On iOS and iPadOS the entry sits in Settings > General > VPN & Device Management on iOS 15 and later. Earlier releases label the same row simply VPN, and some builds add a VPN row directly on the main Settings page once at least one configuration exists.

Inside that screen you get a status toggle and a list of configurations. Tap the info button beside one to see its type, server and account, or use Add VPN Configuration to enter IKEv2, IPsec or L2TP details by hand.

If the screen is empty, no profile has been installed yet, which is normal on a fresh device. The iOS settings hub shows how the General section is laid out across versions.

Android, and why the path keeps moving

Stock Android puts VPN under Settings > Network & internet > VPN, sometimes one level deeper behind an Advanced or More heading. Samsung devices move it to Connections, then More connection settings, then VPN, which is the single most common reason people cannot find it.

The fastest route on any Android build is the search box at the top of Settings: type VPN and jump straight there. The gear icon beside a saved profile holds the useful extras, including Always-on VPN and Block connections without VPN, which stops traffic leaking when the tunnel drops.

Layout differences between manufacturers are covered in the Android hub and in the iOS versus Android settings comparison.

macOS, Windows 11 and Ubuntu

macOS Ventura and later give VPN its own item in System Settings, just below Network. On Monterey and earlier, VPN connections are added as interfaces inside the Network pane using the plus button.

Windows 11 keeps it at Settings > Network & internet > VPN, where Add VPN asks for a provider, connection name, server address and sign-in type. A VPN button also appears in Quick Settings once a connection exists.

Ubuntu and other GNOME desktops list VPN as a section inside Settings > Network, with a plus button offering the protocols your installed plugins support, such as OpenVPN or WireGuard. Imported .ovpn or .conf files land in the same list, as the Ubuntu hub shows.

System profile versus the VPN app's own switch

A system VPN profile is a configuration the operating system owns. You created it, you can edit every field, and the OS toggle connects and disconnects it.

A commercial VPN app is different. It installs a profile you usually cannot edit, then drives it from inside the app, choosing servers and protocols for you. Turning the system toggle off disconnects the tunnel, but the app may reconnect the moment you open it, which looks like the setting ignoring you.

The practical rule: if you subscribed to a service, control it in its app. If your employer or school sent you server details, use the built-in settings, and check the security settings guide for the profile and certificate prompts that come with it.

Confirming the tunnel is actually up

Every platform shows a badge. iOS and iPadOS print VPN in the status bar; Android 12 and later show a small key icon; macOS adds a VPN item to the menu bar; Windows marks the network as Connected in Quick Settings; GNOME lists the active VPN in the system menu.

No badge means no tunnel, even if the app claims otherwise. Check that the underlying connection works first, since a VPN cannot start without one, which the Wi-Fi guide covers.

  • iOS hides VPN under General; Samsung hides it under Connections
  • System profiles you can edit; app-installed profiles you cannot
  • Status bar badge is the only proof the tunnel is really up

Frequently asked questions

Why does my iPhone have no VPN row in Settings?

The top-level VPN row only appears once a configuration exists. Until then, look in General and either VPN or VPN & Device Management, depending on your iOS version.

Does turning off the system toggle stop my VPN app?

It disconnects the current tunnel, but many apps reconnect automatically when opened or when always-on is enabled. Disable the connection inside the app for a lasting result.

What does Always-on VPN do on Android?

It reconnects the chosen VPN whenever the device has a network. Paired with Block connections without VPN, it prevents traffic going out while the tunnel is down.
