The Android Settings menu, explained section by section

Android splits its settings into a short list of blocks, each owning one concern. Once you know which block owns what, the tap sequence stops mattering.

The blocks Android is built from

Stock Android groups settings into named blocks rather than one long roll. On a Pixel the order runs Network & internet, Connected devices, Apps, Notifications, Battery, Storage, Sound & vibration, Display, Wallpaper & style, Accessibility, Security & privacy, Location, Safety & emergency, Passwords & accounts, Digital Wellbeing, Google, System and About phone.

Each block owns one concern, and almost every toggle sits one or two taps inside the block whose name describes it. Learn the labels once and you rarely need to hunt. Every supported release is browsable on the Android hub.

Network, connected devices and apps

Network & internet holds Wi-Fi, the mobile network, SIMs and eSIMs, hotspot and tethering, data saver and airplane mode. That is where you go to join a network or activate an eSIM.

Connected devices is a separate block covering Bluetooth pairing, cast, printing and nearby-device features, so pairing headphones starts there rather than in Network. Apps is the per-app drawer: default apps, unused-app hibernation, special access such as installing unknown apps, and the permission and storage pages for each installed app.

Notifications, battery and storage

Notifications is system-wide, not just a per-app list. It holds notification history, conversation priority, bubbles and the rules for what appears on the lock screen, with the full app list underneath, which is the starting point for taming alerts.

Battery reports drain by app and holds adaptive battery and saver modes, both covered in the battery guide. Storage breaks usage down by category and offers the cleanup tools you want when space runs out.

Security, privacy and System

Security & privacy was two separate sections until Android 13 merged them. It now covers screen lock and biometrics, Find My Device, Google Play Protect, app permissions, the permission manager, the privacy dashboard, and the camera and microphone kill switches.

System is the leftovers drawer: languages and input, gestures, date and time, backup, developer options and reset. On most builds the system update entry lives in System, though several manufacturers move it into About phone or give it a Software update block of its own.

Why your phone looks different

Almost no phone ships with the stock list untouched. Samsung One UI opens with Connections instead of Network & internet, merges battery and storage into Device care, and parks language, keyboard, date and reset under General management. Xiaomi, OPPO and others renumber the list again in their own way.

The underlying settings are usually the same; the labels and grouping are what move. That is why the version and the skin matter so much when you follow a written path, and why comparing Android with iOS works better section by section than tap by tap.

The search bar saves you

Every modern Android build puts a search bar at the top of Settings. It indexes individual toggles, not just section names, so typing a word such as hotspot or vibration lands you on the exact screen even when the manufacturer renamed the parent block.

Search is the reliable route on an unfamiliar phone. If you would rather walk the tree, the Android structure in the simulator is extracted directly from the open-source code, as recorded on the data provenance page, and you can open the Android Settings menu in the simulator to explore any version.

Key points

  • Android groups settings into named blocks, each owning one concern.
  • Skins such as One UI rename and regroup, but the settings still exist.
  • The Settings search bar indexes individual toggles, not just sections.

Frequently asked questions

Why do written Android instructions rarely match my phone?

Most guides describe stock Android on a Pixel. Manufacturer skins rename and regroup sections, so the setting exists but sits under a different label.

Where is the system update on Android?

On stock builds it sits under System. Samsung and several other makers give it a top-level Software update entry or place it inside About phone.

Is Security and privacy one section or two?

Android 13 merged the old separate Security and Privacy sections into one. Older releases and some skins still list them apart.
