How to manage notifications without silencing everything

Turning an app off entirely is the blunt fix, and it is why people miss the one alert that mattered. Modern systems let you keep the important alerts loud and push everything else into a queue you read on your own schedule.

Why the on-off switch is the wrong tool

Most apps send several kinds of notification through one identity. A delivery app sends order updates and marketing; a bank sends transaction alerts and product offers; a chat app sends direct messages and group noise. Switching the app off kills all of them, so the useful ones disappear with the junk and the app quietly gets reinstalled a week later.

Every current system solves this with per-category control plus a scheduling layer. The categories decide what can interrupt at all; the schedule decides when. Learning both takes about ten minutes and removes most of the pressure to declare notification bankruptcy.

Android channels are the real control

Since Android 8, every notification an app sends must belong to a channel, and each channel has its own importance level, sound and badge behaviour. Open an app's entry in the Apps section of Settings and its Notifications page lists those channels by name, which is where you turn off promotions while keeping order updates.

The fast route is a long press on any notification as it arrives: the panel that appears controls the exact channel that alert came from. Android also separates Default from Silent delivery, gives Conversations their own priority tier, and lets Do Not Disturb allow specific people and apps through. Android 13 added a permission prompt, so new apps must ask before they can notify you at all. The Android settings hub shows how the pages nest.

iOS: delivery style, Summary and Focus

On iPhone and iPad, the Notifications pane lists every app and lets you choose where its alerts appear: Lock Screen, Notification Centre, banners, or a combination. Turning off banners but keeping Notification Centre is the quiet middle ground for apps you want to check rather than be interrupted by.

Scheduled Summary, added in iOS 15, collects low-priority apps and delivers them in one batch at times you pick, while anything marked Time Sensitive still comes straight through. Focus modes go further by defining allowed people and apps per context, and Focus filters can even narrow which account an app shows. Focus syncs across your Apple devices, so setting it once on the phone covers the Mac and iPad too.

Desktop rules on Windows and macOS

Windows 11 groups this under System, in Notifications, where you can set do not disturb, choose which apps count as priority, and schedule quiet hours automatically during games, full-screen apps or a chosen time window. Windows 10 called the same feature Focus assist and put it in a slightly different place.

macOS mirrors the iPhone model with a Notifications pane and shared Focus modes, plus a switch for whether alerts appear while the screen is mirrored or shared. Both desktops treat browser notifications separately, so a site you allowed months ago is disabled in the browser's own site permissions, not the OS. The macOS versus Windows comparison covers where the two systems diverge.

A triage method that holds up

Sort every app into three tiers. Interrupt is for people and time-critical systems: calls, messages from named contacts, calendar, two-factor codes, home security. Review is silent delivery with no sound or badge, read when you open the shade. Off is everything else, including anything that has ever sent you a coupon.

Do one pass now, then a two-minute cleanup whenever an alert annoys you: act on the notification, then immediately demote its channel. Fewer background wake-ups also helps battery life. For the current menu wording on each platform, use the notifications guide and the settings finder, or open the real menus in the settings simulator and click through them first.

Key points

  • Android channels let you keep order updates and block promotions
  • iOS Scheduled Summary batches low-priority apps into set delivery times
  • Sort apps into interrupt, review and off, then demote as they annoy you

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Do Not Disturb and Focus?

Do Not Disturb silences everything except your exceptions. Focus is a set of named modes, each with its own allowed people, apps and even home screen, and iOS treats Do Not Disturb as one of them.

Why do some notifications still arrive during Do Not Disturb?

Alarms, repeated calls from the same number and alerts marked time sensitive or critical are allowed through by design. Those exceptions are configurable in the same pane.

Do notifications drain the battery?

The push message itself is tiny, but each one wakes the screen and often the app behind it. Cutting low-value alerts gives a small but real improvement.
