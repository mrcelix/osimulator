The iOS Settings app, explained top to bottom

The iOS Settings app looks like one endless list, but it is built from a handful of predictable blocks. Learn the blocks and you stop scrolling blindly for the toggle you need.

How the list is organised

The iOS Settings app is a single scrolling list rather than a grid of panels. The order is deliberate: your account sits at the top, radios and connections come next, then system-wide behaviour, and finally the settings that belong to individual apps.

Apple reshuffles this list every few releases, so a path that worked on iOS 15 may sit one level deeper on iOS 18. Rather than memorising paths, learn the blocks. Once you know which block owns a setting, finding it takes seconds. Each supported release is browsable on the iOS hub.

The Apple Account card and the connectivity block

The card at the very top carries your name and photo. Tapping it opens everything tied to your Apple Account: iCloud storage and which apps sync to it, Family Sharing, Find My, payment methods, and the list of devices signed in to the same account.

Directly beneath it sits the connectivity block: Airplane Mode, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Mobile Data or Cellular, and Personal Hotspot. A VPN row appears only once a VPN profile exists. This block is where you go to join a network, pair an accessory, or add an eSIM.

Notifications, Focus and Screen Time

The next block governs interruptions. Notifications lists every app that has asked permission, with per-app control over banners, sounds, badges and the lock screen. Sounds & Haptics sits beside it, and Focus holds Do Not Disturb along with any custom modes you build.

Screen Time is in the same block. It reports usage, sets app limits, and holds Content & Privacy Restrictions, which is the real parental controls panel. If alerts have got out of hand, the notification guide works through trimming them app by app.

General is the system drawer

General is the largest section and carries the least descriptive name in the whole app. It holds About, Software Update, iPhone Storage, Background App Refresh, Date & Time, Keyboard, Language & Region, VPN & Device Management, and Transfer or Reset iPhone.

Treat General as the drawer for anything that concerns the device as a whole rather than one feature. Updates live here, which is why the update guide starts in General, and so does the storage screen you need when the phone runs out of space.

Per-app settings at the bottom

Keep scrolling past Display & Brightness, Accessibility, Face ID & Passcode, Battery and Privacy & Security and you reach app settings. Apple apps come first, then third-party apps in alphabetical order.

What appears here is only what the developer chose to expose, plus the permission switches iOS adds automatically: location, camera, microphone, contacts, notifications and mobile data. Many apps keep their real options inside the app itself, so a thin panel is normal. On iOS 18 Apple gathered these entries under a single Apps row instead of leaving them loose at the end of the list.

Search beats scrolling

Pull down on the top of the Settings list and a search field appears. It matches section names and individual toggles, so typing a word such as keyboard or storage jumps straight to the right screen without you guessing the hierarchy.

Search is also the fastest way to survive a redesign, since a label usually outlives the path it sits on. If you want to see where the same option lands on another platform, the iOS and Android comparison maps the equivalents, and you can open a real iOS Settings menu in the simulator to practise without touching your phone.

Key points

  • Settings is ordered account, connectivity, system behaviour, then apps.
  • General is the catch-all drawer for updates, storage, language and reset.
  • Pull down to search when a label moved between iOS versions.

Frequently asked questions

Why does an app show almost nothing in Settings?

Developers choose how much to expose there. Many apps keep their options in their own in-app settings screen, leaving only the system permission switches in the Settings list.

Where did Software Update go?

It sits inside General, not at the top level. On recent releases the same screen also holds automatic update options and beta update channels.

Does the Settings order change between iPhone models?

Model differences are small, but iOS version differences are real. Rows appear, merge or move between releases, so check the section name rather than a memorised tap sequence.
