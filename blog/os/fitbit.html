How Fitbit OS organises Settings

The order is a priority list. Bluetooth first, because a watch that is not paired does very little; then Notifications, Display, Exercise and Activity, Heart and Health, Vibration and Audio, Clock Face, Fitbit Wallet, Battery and About. Sensor features and phone features are kept apart, which is unusual. Most wearables mix them together. Here, everything the watch measures about you sits under Heart and Health, and everything the phone pushes at you sits under Notifications. That split makes the menu easy to reason about even when you are tapping with one finger while walking.

Three levels is the limit, and most branches stop at two. Heart and Health is the widest, holding Heart rate, High/low HR alerts, SpO2, Stress management (EDA), Sleep, ECG app and Skin temperature, which is really a list of the sensors the hardware carries. Vibration and Audio takes the opposite approach, offering Normal, Strong, Vibrate on wrist and Chimes and nothing more. Where people get lost is the boundary with the phone app: several things that feel like watch settings are only adjustable on the phone, and the watch menu gives no hint of that.

Three settings worth finding

  • Settings > Heart and Health > High/low HR alerts

    The watch can warn you when resting heart rate sits outside a range you set, which is one of the few settings on the device that people are told about by a clinician rather than discovering for themselves. Finding it takes two taps once you know the branch.

  • Settings > Display > Always-on display

    Leaving the screen lit costs battery, and this is the single switch that decides it. It sits with Screen timeout and Screen wake, so the three things that govern when the display is awake stay together rather than scattered, which is worth seeing before you change any of them.

  • Settings > Notifications > Quick replies

    Canned responses to text messages are configured here alongside Calls, Calendar and Do Not Disturb. Knowing the option exists changes how usable the watch feels, because it turns a notification you can only dismiss into one you can answer without reaching for a phone.

How this menu got here

Fitbit's watch software grew out of a fitness tracker interface that had almost no settings at all, and each generation of hardware added a menu entry for the sensor it introduced. That is why Heart and Health reads like a hardware inventory. The two builds listed, OS 5 and OS 4, span the period when the health features were consolidated under a single heading and the clock face and wallet entries settled into their current position. The layout is reconstructed from the shipping interface.

Who this is useful for

Someone comparing wearables before buying wants to know how much can be changed on the wrist rather than in a phone app, and this shows it in a minute. Retail staff demonstrating a watch can rehearse the tour. Carers setting up a device for a relative can find the health alert pages first. Anyone who has lost a watch and is describing its settings to support gets an accurate reference.

What is real and what is sample data

The structure is the real thing: menu names, their order, how they are grouped and what sits inside each one. That is why this article can quote a path such as Settings > Heart and Health > High/low HR alerts and expect it to match your watch.

The state cannot be. A device name, an address, a build number, a network name, a storage figure, whether a particular switch is on or off — those belong to a physical watch, not to a menu tree. Those values appear here as clearly-marked samples. They are there so the row has the right shape, and they should never be read as a fact about a real watch.

Questions about Fitbit OS settings

Is the Fitbit OS menu shown here exact?

The structure follows the shipping interface, since this software is not open source and cannot be read from published code. Menu names and their order are reproduced as closely as possible. Everything inside a page that would describe a real device, such as a paired phone name or a battery figure, is a sample value.

Which Fitbit OS versions are available?

OS 5 and OS 4 are both here, and you can switch between them to see what moved. The differences are mostly about where the health features sit and how the top level is ordered, so a side-by-side look answers most questions about whether an older instruction still applies.

Can I change settings on my own watch from here?

No. Nothing in the simulator connects to hardware, and no pairing takes place. It is a map of the menu rather than a remote control. Use it to learn where an option lives, then make the change on the watch itself or in the phone app, whichever the setting belongs to.
