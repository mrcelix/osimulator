How Zepp OS organises Settings

The sections read like a list of jobs the watch actually does. Connection gathers Phone, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, Airplane Mode, Alexa and Find my phone, so everything involving another device sits in one place. Watch Faces handles appearance and the always-on behaviour. Display, Sound and Vibration cover output. Health Monitoring is the section that justifies the hardware, holding heart rate, sleep, stress and blood oxygen tracking. Notifications, Workouts, Battery, System and About finish the list. Nothing is nested for the sake of tidiness, because a hidden row on a watch is effectively a missing row.

Three levels is the ceiling, and most useful controls sit at two. The real design tension shows up in Health Monitoring, where each sensor has its own frequency and threshold options that quietly govern how long the battery lasts. Turning on continuous heart rate and blood oxygen changes the charging routine more than anything in the Battery section does, but the two are not connected in the menu. That split is where newcomers get caught: they look for power settings under Battery and never realise the sensors are the thing draining it.

Three settings worth finding

  • Settings > Health Monitoring > SpO2

    Blood oxygen measurement is the most battery-hungry sensor option on the watch. Whether it samples continuously or only when asked is decided here, and it explains most complaints about a watch that suddenly stopped lasting a week.

  • Settings > Watch Faces > Always On Display

    Keeping the dial lit means you can read the time without moving your arm, at a real cost in runtime. It sits under faces rather than under display, which is why people hunt for it in the wrong section.

  • Settings > Display > Raise wrist to wake

    The gesture that lights the screen when you turn your arm sounds trivial until it fires all night or fails during a workout. Adjusting it is the difference between a watch that feels responsive and one that feels broken.

How this menu got here

Watch settings here grew out of a companion-phone model, where the phone app held most configuration and the wrist showed only what you might change while wearing it. Over time more moved onto the watch itself, particularly around health sensors and notification handling, which is why those sections carry the most rows. The two versions listed, Zepp OS 4 and Zepp OS 3, represent a shift toward doing more on device, so comparing them shows sections gaining depth rather than the top-level list changing shape.

Who this is useful for

Retail staff demonstrating a watch on a busy counter can show the menu on a larger screen instead of holding a tiny dial up to a customer. Runners deciding which tracking to leave on before a long event can plan the whole configuration in advance. Older wearers or anyone helping a relative set one up can rehearse the taps, and reviewers comparing wearables can see what a section actually contains.

What is real and what is sample data

The structure is the real thing: menu names, their order, how they are grouped and what sits inside each one. That is why this article can quote a path such as Settings > Health Monitoring > SpO2 and expect it to match your watch.

The state cannot be. A device name, an address, a build number, a network name, a storage figure, whether a particular switch is on or off — those belong to a physical watch, not to a menu tree. The simulator fills those in with obvious sample values. Use them to learn where a figure lives; look at your own watch to learn what it says.

Questions about Zepp OS settings

Does this work for all Amazfit watches?

The structure follows the platform rather than a single model, so section names and their order match across watches running the same generation. Individual hardware differences mean a model without a particular sensor will not show that row. Sensor readings and battery figures here are sample values, not measurements from anything real.

How was this layout put together?

It is reconstructed from the shipping interface rather than extracted from published source, since the platform is not open source. That means the names and their arrangement follow what the watch displays. Device-specific details such as a paired phone name or a battery percentage are sample values shown so the screens read naturally.

Can I check settings before buying a watch?

That is one of the more common uses. Walking Health Monitoring and Notifications tells you what the watch can track and how much control you get over alerts, which is usually more informative than a specification list. You can also put it beside another wearable using comparison mode.
