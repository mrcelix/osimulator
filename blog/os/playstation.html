Find your way around the PS5 settings menu in a browser

A console settings menu has a different job from a phone's: fewer decisions, made once, usually with a controller from across the room. Sony's fifth-generation machine keeps twelve top-level rows and only three levels of depth, so nothing is buried. Walking that layout in a browser is a quick way to see where saved data, storage and family controls actually live.

How PlayStation organises Settings

Twelve rows, and each one names a thing rather than a category. Users and Accounts, Network, Screen and Video, Sound, Storage, Accessories, System, Accessibility, Notifications, Captures and Broadcasts, Saved Data and Game/App Settings, Family and Parental Controls. The names are long because they are literal; nobody has to guess whether screenshots live under sharing or media when the row says Captures and Broadcasts. That verbosity works on a television, where you read the list from two metres away and a one-word label would be ambiguous. It is the opposite of a phone's terse column.

Depth stops at three levels, which is unusual and welcome. Storage opens straight onto Games and Apps, Media Gallery, Saved Data, Free, Console Storage and M.2 SSD Storage, so the question of what is eating the drive is answered on the second screen. Sound behaves the same way, with Audio Output, Volume, 3D Audio and Adjust Microphone Level side by side. The trade-off is a long second screen in places: Users and Accounts has to carry Account, Profile, Family Management, Link with Other Services and Login Settings between them.

Three settings worth finding

  • Settings > Storage > M.2 SSD Storage

    An expansion drive fitted inside the console gets its own row here, separate from Console Storage, and it is where you move installed games when the built-in drive fills up. Knowing the row exists before you buy a drive saves a lot of guessing about whether the machine will use it.

  • Settings > Sound > 3D Audio

    Positional sound over headphones is one of the few settings that changes how a game plays rather than how it looks. It is a couple of steps from the main list, and it is worth finding before deciding whether a headset upgrade is actually needed.

  • Settings > Users and Accounts > Family Management

    This is where an adult account sets up children's accounts and the limits attached to them, and it sits under accounts rather than under the parental controls row many people try first. Parents setting up a console usually need this screen within the first ten minutes.

How this menu got here

Console settings used to be an afterthought bolted onto a dashboard. As machines gained storage tiers, capture tools and account systems, the menu grew into a proper tree with its own vocabulary. The two versions here, 24.02 and 23.09, are system software updates rather than new generations; comparing them shows how rows get added and renamed between updates without the overall shape changing. The console interface is not open source, so this layout is reconstructed from the shipping menus rather than taken from published code.

Who this is useful for

Parents who want to know what the controls cover before handing over a console at Christmas. Forum moderators writing an answer that has to be right the first time. Anyone comparing a console's audio and video options against a PC before buying. It also helps if you are troubleshooting for a relative by phone and need to describe a row you cannot see.

What is real and what is sample data

The structure is the real thing: menu names, their order, how they are grouped and what sits inside each one. That is why this article can quote a path such as Settings > Storage > M.2 SSD Storage and expect it to match your console.

The state cannot be. A device name, an address, a build number, a network name, a storage figure, whether a particular switch is on or off — those belong to a physical console, not to a menu tree. The simulator fills those in with obvious sample values. Use them to learn where a figure lives; look at your own console to learn what it says.

Questions about PlayStation settings

Do I need a PlayStation to use this?

No. The simulator runs in a browser tab on any machine, with a console-style frame and controller-like navigation. It does not connect to a console, does not read anything from your account, and does not need one. Values such as free space or a signed-in name are sample text rather than a reading from real hardware.

Is this an official Sony product?

It is not. The site has no affiliation with Sony and is not endorsed by any vendor whose interface it reproduces. What it offers is a careful reconstruction of the menu layout, built so people can learn the structure, compare versions and write accurate instructions without needing the hardware in front of them.

Can I find a setting if I only know its name?

Yes. A search across all the systems in the collection tells you which one hides a given setting and where it sits, which is handy when you remember the wording but not the menu. From there you can open that system directly and follow the same path a player would take on the machine.
