Look inside the OnePlus take on Android settings

OnePlus builds its phone software on Android but rearranges the front page, and the result is a shorter list with more inside each row. Twelve top-level entries cover about 233 settings across four levels. If you are weighing this against stock Android or a Samsung handset, reading the tree side by side is quicker than reading a review.

How OxygenOS organises Settings

Two rows sit at the top on their own, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, because those are the ones people open daily. Everything else is bundled: Connection and sharing takes SIM and network, Hotspot, Airplane mode, NFC, Cast, OnePlus Share, VPN and Private DNS in a single row. Customization is given equal billing with Display and brightness, which says a lot about the priorities here, since Wallpapers and style, Icons, Fingerprint animation, Horizon Light and Always-On Display are treated as settings rather than decoration. Special features, Battery, Security and privacy, System settings and About device close the list.

The bundling makes the first screen short and the second screen dense. Display and brightness alone carries Light, Dark, Auto brightness, Screen refresh rate, Ultra HDR, Screen color mode, Aqua Touch and Text and display size, so the row you want is usually one level down rather than three. That is friendlier than a deep tree but harder to scan, and the naming does not always help: Special features is a genuine catch-all whose contents you have to open to learn. Against stock Android the vocabulary is familiar; the grouping is not.

Three settings worth finding

  • Settings > Customization > Horizon Light

    An edge-lighting effect that glows around the curve of the screen when something arrives, useful on a phone lying face down on a desk. It is filed as customisation rather than notification behaviour, which is why people who go looking under alerts never find it.

  • Settings > Display and brightness > Screen refresh rate

    Choosing between a smoother, hungrier screen and a longer-lasting battery is one of the few settings with a visible effect on both feel and runtime. It sits a single level down, and trying each option is the fastest way to decide which you prefer.

  • Settings > Connection and sharing > Private DNS

    Routing name lookups through a chosen provider changes how the phone resolves addresses, which matters for filtering or for privacy on shared networks. Being buried inside a broad connectivity row means most owners never see it, even though it takes one screen to set.

How this menu got here

The software began as a lightly modified Android build with a reputation for staying close to Google's version. Over time it absorbed more of a sibling platform's design and gained its own customisation layer, which is why Customization now sits near the top rather than buried under display options. Versions 15 and 14 are both available in the simulator, and switching between them shows which rows were renamed or regrouped in that transition. The interface layer is not open source, so it is reconstructed from the shipping menus.

Who this is useful for

People cross-shopping OnePlus against Pixel or Samsung can compare front pages directly instead of trusting a spec sheet. Refurbished sellers writing a listing can check which features a version actually exposes. Trainers running a device-handover session get a projectable copy of the menu. And anyone who has just unboxed a handset can plan the first ten changes before switching it on.

What is real and what is sample data

The structure is the real thing: menu names, their order, how they are grouped and what sits inside each one. That is why this article can quote a path such as Settings > Customization > Horizon Light and expect it to match your phone.

The state cannot be. A device name, an address, a build number, a network name, a storage figure, whether a particular switch is on or off — those belong to a physical phone, not to a menu tree. The simulator fills those in with obvious sample values. Use them to learn where a figure lives; look at your own phone to learn what it says.

Questions about OxygenOS settings

Is OxygenOS just Android with a new skin?

It is built on Android, but the arrangement differs enough that instructions written for a Pixel often send you to the wrong row. Grouping is the main change: fewer top-level entries, more inside each one, and a customisation section given its own place. Comparing the two trees side by side in the simulator shows the gap quickly.

Will the Wi-Fi networks shown be mine?

No. Network names, paired accessories, storage figures and build numbers belong to a physical handset and cannot be read by a browser page anyway. They appear as clearly marked sample values so screens look realistic. The parts that are accurate are the menu titles, their order and how deeply each one nests.

Can I use this to write support instructions?

That is one of the main uses. Because the row names and their order match the shipping interface, a path you write from the simulator matches what a caller sees. You can also record the click-path and share it, so the person on the other end follows the same taps instead of parsing a paragraph.
